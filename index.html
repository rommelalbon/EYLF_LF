module one

Three words that hold the whole framework

Before we look at outcomes and checklists, the EYLF asks us to see each child through these three lenses. Read each card. Then notice which one you lean into most — and which one might need a little more love in your room.

B

Belonging

Knowing where and with whom you belong. The child is interdependent — held inside family, community, place and culture. Trusting relationships are the soil everything grows from.

B

Being

The right to be a child right now — to seek, sense, play and rest. Childhood is not only preparation for tomorrow; it is also a meaningful today.

B

Becoming

The rapid change of early childhood — identities, capabilities and relationships in motion. We grow alongside children as citizens, not just pupils-in-training.

module three

The 8 Principles — the why behind what we do

Click any tab. Then jump to Activity 1 where you'll match a real classroom moment to the principle it draws on. (Spoiler: most moments touch several!)

Secure, Respectful & Reciprocal Relationships

Children's first attachments — and a widening network of trusted educators — are the secure base from which they explore. Healthy brain development is shaped by warm, attuned interactions, predictable routines, and the small rituals that say "you belong here, you are seen."

In practice

Greet each child by name in their preferred language. Pause for unhurried morning hand-offs. Hold a consistent key-educator for under-3s.

You'll know it's working when…

A child seeks you out for comfort and for shared discoveries. They venture further, knowing they can return.

Partnerships

Families are the child's first and most influential teachers. Genuine partnership means valuing what families know, sharing decisions, communicating safely, and acting with empathy when families are facing adversity.

In practice

Two-way "all about me" forms at enrolment. Translate key messages. Invite family contributions to curriculum, not just to events.

Watch for

One-way newsletters dressed up as "partnership." True partnership invites response — and shifts the plan.

Respect for Diversity

Every child arrives with a family culture, language, heritage and lifestyle that belongs in the curriculum — not as decoration on a wall, but as living material for play and learning.

In practice

Audit your books and dolls: do they reflect every child in the room? Invite families to share a song, a recipe, a celebration.

Avoid

Tokenism — a single "culture week." Embed diversity in everyday routines, language and resources.

Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander Perspectives

Embedding First Nations perspectives is a shared responsibility for all services — regardless of whether ATSI children are enrolled. It honours 60,000+ years of knowledge and advances Reconciliation.

In practice

Acknowledgement of Country in your own words. Use local Aboriginal names for seasons, places, animals. Engage local Elders respectfully.

Deeper move

Build an "intercultural space" — where Western and Aboriginal ways of knowing sit side-by-side, not one inside the other.

Equity, Inclusion & High Expectations

Equitable means fair, not equal. Some children need more — more time, more scaffolding, more reasonable adjustments — to access the same learning. High expectations apply to every child.

In practice

Make reasonable adjustments under the Disability Standards. Plan multiple entry points to the same experience.

Mindset shift

From "What's wrong with this child?" → "What barriers in my environment can I remove?"

Sustainability

EYLF V2.0 broadens sustainability across three dimensions: environmental, social and economic. Children are powerful advocates for change.

In practice

The 7Rs — reduce, reuse, recycle, respect, repair, reflect, refuse. Compost. Worm farm. Living gardens.

Beyond nature

Sustainability is also fairness, sharing, citizenship. Talk about who gets what, and why.

Critical Reflection & Ongoing Professional Learning

Reflection asks "what happened?"; critical reflection asks "who benefited? who was silenced? what would I do differently and why?" — drawing on theory, ethics and diverse perspectives.

Try these prompts

Who is advantaged when I plan this way? Who is excluded? What theory am I leaning on without naming?

Make it routine

10 minutes at the end of every Friday with a colleague. One observation, three perspectives.

Collaborative Leadership & Teamwork

Every educator leads — through their ethical practice with children and through respectful, professional conversations with colleagues. Children sense the work culture of the room.

In practice

Peer mentoring. Share new learning at team meetings. Contribute to the Quality Improvement Plan.

Tone

Curiosity over judgement. "Help me see what you saw" beats "you should have…"

module four

The 7 Practices — the how

If Principles answer why, Practices answer how. These are the moves you'll be observed making, and the moves we'll plan together.

Holistic, Integrated & Interconnected Approaches

Children's learning is woven — physical, cognitive, social, emotional, spiritual and creative threads, all at once. Plan and observe with the whole child in view, not just the "literacy slot."

Try this

Pick one play episode this week. Map which Outcomes it touched — you'll be surprised how many.

Don't

Don't reduce a child to a checklist. The list serves the child, not the other way around.

Responsiveness to Children

Notice the strengths, languages and "funds of knowledge" each child carries from home. Use open questions, follow-up prompts and "teachable moments" — planned and spontaneous.

Micro-moves

"Tell me more." "What if we…?" Pause. Wait 5 full seconds before adding your idea.

Bigger move

Adjust tomorrow's plan based on what you noticed today. That is responsiveness.

Play-Based Learning & Intentionality

Play is the context and the process. Educators are intentional — choosing when to observe, when to enter the play, when to model, question, scaffold, or step back.

Balance

Mix child-led, adult-initiated and adult-guided experiences across each day. Document the balance.

Intentional teaching

Modelling, demonstrating, asking open questions, sustained shared thinking, naming concepts.

Learning Environments

Physical, temporal, social and intellectual — all four. Outdoor and indoor. Active and calm. Reflective of every child's culture. Accessible for every body.

Audit prompt

Stand at the door at child-height. What invites you in? What signals belonging? What signals exclusion?

Outdoors

Loose parts, real risk, sustained time. Outdoors is not a break from learning — it is learning.

Cultural Responsiveness

More than awareness — a three-way ongoing process between educator, child and family. Honours diverse ways of communicating, includes ATSI perspectives, and acts against discrimination.

Start with self

Name your own cultural assumptions. The shoes you stand in shape the floor you see.

Make it visible

Home languages on labels. Family photos at child height. Music from every family represented.

Continuity of Learning & Transitions

Transitions are big (home → kindy, kindy → Pre-Primary) and small (snack → group time). Both need rituals, predictability and shared information across settings.

Hand-overs

Share a child's strengths, languages and interests with the Pre-Primary teacher — not just their "areas of concern."

Daily rituals

Same tidy-up song. Same goodbye gesture. Small certainties hold big feelings.

Assessment & Evaluation

Three lenses: assessment for learning (formative — informs next steps), of learning (summative — shows distance travelled), and as learning (the child reflects on their own learning).

Methods

Learning stories, jottings, photos, work samples, child voice quotes, family input, floorbooks.

Evaluate too

Don't only assess the child — evaluate your plan. Did it work? For whom? What's next?

module five

The 5 Learning Outcomes — the what

Each outcome is broad and observable. They are not a checklist of milestones — they are lenses for noticing the integrated learning already happening in your room. Click any outcome to open it up.

Identity is the cornerstone. It is built through trusting relationships, agency, and feeling recognised. Identity is not fixed — it changes across settings.

Children feel…
  • Safe, secure and supported
  • Emerging autonomy, resilience and agency
  • Knowledgeable, confident self-identities
  • Able to interact with care, empathy & respect
Educators…
  • Acknowledge cues, signals and home languages
  • Build culturally safe places to share story & culture
  • Hold high expectations of every child
  • Mediate and name feelings, model empathy

From family to community to the digital and natural world — children grow as active, informed citizens with reciprocal rights and responsibilities.

Children develop…
  • Sense of connectedness & citizenship
  • Response to diversity with respect
  • Awareness of fairness
  • Social responsibility & respect for the environment
Educators…
  • Connect setting to local community
  • Embed the 7Rs of sustainability
  • Model anti-bias responses to unfairness
  • Build authentic ATSI community partnerships

Wellbeing is physical and psychological — resilience, self-regulation, healthy bodies, healthy minds, and the right to feel happy and safe.

Children become strong in…
  • Social, emotional & mental wellbeing
  • Physical learning and wellbeing
  • Strategies for their own health & safety
  • Body autonomy & consent
Educators…
  • Adopt trauma-informed practice
  • Teach body safety in age-appropriate ways
  • Plan for risky play with reasonable challenge
  • Model self-regulation language & strategies

A growth mindset, persistence, problem-solving and the ability to transfer learning from one context to another — built on curiosity and hands-on investigation.

Children develop…
  • Curiosity, persistence, creativity, reflexivity
  • Problem solving, inquiry, hypothesising
  • Ability to transfer & adapt learning
  • Resourcing learning through people & materials
Educators…
  • Praise effort & process over outcome
  • Model wonder & "I don't know yet — let's find out"
  • Scaffold with open-ended questions
  • Make learning visible & revisitable

Communication is far more than spoken English — it's gesture, art, music, dance, digital media, signs, symbols, home languages and emerging literacy and numeracy.

Children…
  • Interact verbally and non-verbally for many purposes
  • Engage with and gain meaning from a range of texts
  • Express ideas through diverse media
  • Begin to understand symbols & pattern systems
  • Use digital technologies safely & creatively
Educators…
  • Value and use home languages alongside English
  • Read widely, sing, rhyme, tell stories every day
  • Provide rich mathematical vocabulary & play
  • Model safe, purposeful use of digital tools

How the activities work

You'll meet five formats: matching, multiple choice, scenario reflection, planning, and a final self-audit. Each one has an answer reveal so you can check yourself.

2 Pick one Choose the most EYLF-aligned response
Four-year-old Liam is upset at drop-off every morning this week. He clings to his father, cries, and won't engage with the play set-up until 30 minutes after his dad leaves. Which response best reflects EYLF Principle 1 (Secure, respectful and reciprocal relationships) and Practice 6 (Continuity and transitions)?
3 Pick one Identify the Outcome at work
During outdoor play, Mei and Joshua argue over a single bucket of water. Joshua suggests they "share by taking turns counting to ten." Mei agrees, but then offers a better idea: "Or we get another bucket so we both have one!" They run together to find a second bucket. Which Learning Outcome is most strongly evidenced here?
4 Reflect Embedding ATSI perspectives — your first move
Your Pre-Primary classroom in Laos follows the Western Australian curriculum. A colleague says, “Since our students are Lao and international students, we don’t really need to focus on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives this term.” How would you respectfully respond to your colleague, and what is one practical first step you could take next week to help students build respect and understanding of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultures while also connecting learning to the students’ own cultural backgrounds?

Write your answer below:

Sample response: The Western Australian curriculum and EYLF make it clear that embedding Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives is important in all learning settings, even in international schools outside Australia. Although our students are mainly Lao and international students, learning about First Nations cultures helps children develop respect, inclusivity, and intercultural understanding. It also encourages students to reflect on and value their own cultural identities and traditions.

One concrete first step could be adding picture books, music, and artwork by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander creators to our classroom environment and using them during circle time next week. For example, students could listen to a Dreaming story and then discuss similarities with Lao storytelling traditions, respect for nature, family connections, or community values. From there, we can gradually build deeper cultural understanding through art activities, songs, cultural discussions, and comparisons between Australian Indigenous cultures and the students’ own backgrounds.
5 Plan it Run one play episode through the planning cycle

Pick one real play episode from your room this week. Write a short note for each stage. There are no wrong answers — this is your draft.

① Observe — what did you notice?

② Analyse Learning — what's happening, against which Outcome(s)?

③ Plan — what's a next step?

④ Implement — how will you enact it tomorrow?

⑤ Reflect / Evaluate — how will you know it worked?

6 Pick one Assessment — for, of, or as learning?
At the end of a week-long mini-project on "where our food comes from," you sit with the children at the floorbook and ask: "What did you learn? What surprised you? What do you still wonder about?" The children dictate, draw, and you scribe their reflections.

What kind of assessment is this?
7 Self-check My room, my practice — an honest stocktake

For each prompt, write the one specific change you could realistically make in the next two weeks. Be concrete. ("Add a basket of mirrors at the family corner" beats "be more inclusive.")
